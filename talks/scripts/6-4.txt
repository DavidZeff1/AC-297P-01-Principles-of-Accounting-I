[warmly] Today's topic is small, literally. It's about petty cash.

Some payments are just too small to bother writing a check. A few dollars of postage due. Reimbursing an employee who grabbed some office supplies. For those, companies keep a petty cash fund, also called an imprest fund.

Setting it up is easy. The company writes a check to cash, say for two hundred dollars, cashes it, and puts the money in a petty cash box. One person, the custodian, is responsible for it. The entry is: debit Petty Cash, credit Cash. That just moves part of the company's cash into a separate little account.

Now, the rules. Company policy says what petty cash can be used for. And every time money comes out, a receipt goes in: a petty cash voucher showing the amount and what it was for. [pause] So here's the key check. At any moment, the cash in the box, plus the vouchers, should equal the fund's balance. Two hundred dollars, always.

When the cash starts running low, the fund gets replenished. A check is written to bring the box back up to its original two hundred. The vouchers come out, and they're recorded as expenses: postage expense, supplies expense, and so on, with a credit to Cash. And here's the part people often miss: the Petty Cash account itself doesn't change when you replenish. It stays at two hundred.

What if the box comes up a few dollars short, or has a little extra? Maybe someone made change wrong. The difference goes to an account called Cash Short and Over. A shortage is a debit, which acts like an expense. An overage is a credit, which acts like revenue. Retailers use this same account every day: if the register drawer has more or less cash than the recorded sales, the difference goes there too.

Finally, as a company grows, it might need a bigger fund. Increasing it looks just like setting it up: debit Petty Cash, credit Cash for the increase. In fact, the only times you ever touch the Petty Cash account are when you create the fund or change its size.

[warmly] So, the takeaways. Set up petty cash with a debit to Petty Cash and a credit to Cash, and give it a custodian. Cash plus vouchers always equals the fund. Replenishing records the expenses, not a change to Petty Cash. And small differences go to Cash Short and Over. See you next lesson.

Accounting is learned by doing, not by reading. Every day follows the same rhythm: learn a lesson, then prove it to yourself by working it without looking. Tape this page where you study.

A study day (5 hours)

0:00 – 0:10Warm-up. Practice → Flashcards → yesterday’s chapter, with Only cards I’m still learning checked. Clear as many as you can.10 min
0:10 – 1:40Lessons, back to back (about 45 min each, see the loop below). Usually 2 lessons in this block.90 min
1:40 – 1:50Break: stand up, water, no phone scrolling.10 min
1:50 – 3:20Lessons, 2 more.90 min
3:20 – 3:30Break.10 min
3:30 – 4:30Last lesson(s) of the day. If you finish early, redo the hardest example from today on paper.60 min
4:30 – 5:00Lock it in. Practice → Flashcards → today’s lessons (mark Got it / Still learning). Then Practice → Multiple choice → today’s chapter → 10 questions. Write your misses in your notebook.30 min

The lesson loop (≈45 min per lesson)

  1. Listen to the audio lesson first (3–4 min) for the big picture.
  2. Read the lesson. Before revealing any example, predict the debit and credit or the number on paper.
  3. Work every interactive example yourself, then check.
  4. Practice check: 75% or better marks the lesson done. Below that, reread and retake.
  5. One line in your notebook: the main idea in your own words.

Rules that make it stick

1Paper first. Write the entry or formula before checking the answer.
2Never skip a miss. Every wrong answer gets redone until it’s right.
3DEAD / RELIC every day until it’s automatic.
4Same time daily. Consistency beats long weekend cramming.
5Phone away during lesson blocks.

Review & exam day (5 hours + the exam)

Hour 1Find your gaps. Practice → Multiple choice → each chapter of the unit → All questions. List every topic you missed.
Hours 2–3Fix them. Reopen the lesson behind each miss and redo its examples on paper, answer hidden. Then Retry the ones I missed until all are right.
Hour 4Flashcards: the whole unit with Only cards I’m still learning until the pile is empty.
Hour 5Dress rehearsal: one mixed set of 50 questions across the unit. 90% or better → take the exam. Below 90% → repeat hours 2–3 and take it tomorrow.

Pace

7 study days a week → about 2½ weeks.
5 days a week → about 3½ weeks.
3 days a week → about 6 weeks.

If you fall behind

Don’t skip the lock-in block or a review day. Slide the plan a day instead. A lesson you rushed costs more on the exam than the day you saved.

On exam day

  • Journal entries: sketch the T-accounts or write dr / cr first, then check that debits equal credits.
  • Calculations: write the formula before the numbers. It catches most mistakes and can earn partial credit.
  • Read the trap words: book value vs fair value · stated rate vs market rate · issued vs outstanding · earned vs received.
  • Stuck? Mark it, move on, come back. Don’t let one question eat your time.
